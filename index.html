Two questions, one heart

Made for mission

A starting point, not a label

The five “love languages” were popularized by Gary Chapman. Research suggests we all need all five — treat your result as a conversation and prayer starter, not a diagnosis.

🕊️ Receive more openly

🙏 The one everybody gets

Pray for the living and the dead. This work of mercy belongs to all five languages — it's how you love people you can't reach. If intercession is where you come alive, that's not a love language at all; that's likely a charism — a grace given for others (1 Cor 12:7). Bring it to your prayer-warrior team, not to this quiz.

💬 Bring it to your household

  1. Give: Where does my top giving language already show up at home — and who near me speaks a language I rarely give?
  2. Receive: When someone loves me in my language, do I receive it — or deflect it? What would openness look like this week?
  3. Gifts: Love languages are natural temperament. Charisms are graces the Spirit gives for others. How might God want to elevate the way I naturally love into a gift for the Church's mission?
  4. Mercy: Which work of mercy could our household actually start this quarter — and who's bringing a friend?
